# Marketing content standards

## Truth before promotion

Marketing must describe behavior users can verify in the current product. A registry record, roadmap item, visual example, source lead, or navigable shell is not automatically an implemented feature.

Use the canonical maturity vocabulary internally and translate it accurately for public audiences. Never collapse all statuses into “available.”

## Claims requiring evidence

Confirm and cite claims about history, architecture, law, permits, codes, safety, accessibility requirements, climate, prices, products, availability, funding, waqf, scholarly positions, and environmental performance.

For changing information, record the observation date and re-check before publication.

## Required boundaries

- Permit pathways are likely applicable and must be verified with the responsible authority or professional.
- Structural, civil, fire, mechanical, electrical, plumbing, accessibility, energy, and construction outputs remain conceptual until qualified review.
- Costs need geography, currency, date, source, maturity, confidence, and exclusions.
- Product records are candidates or observations, not endorsements, verified vendors, quotes, or availability claims.
- Religious content needs source metadata and content review.

## Respectful religious language

- Do not use Qur’anic text, the names of Allah, the shahada, or sacred calligraphy as decorative filler.
- Do not invent hadith paraphrases or attach religious authority to product claims.
- Distinguish a cited religious source from a technical building requirement.
- Seek qualified content review for substantive religious claims and translation.

## Architecture and cultural context

- Name a specific lineage, region, period, source, and limitation where possible.
- Avoid “authentic Islamic design” as an unsupported universal claim.
- Credit craft and cultural authorship.
- Do not present generated geometry as a historic reconstruction.
- Do not reduce a living tradition to a surface style.

## Privacy

- Use neutral project names and anonymous site labels.
- Do not publish personal contacts, precise private locations, donor records, negotiations, quotes, credentials, committee documents, or private exports.
- Obtain informed permission for identifiable people, projects, and testimonials.
- Remove image metadata and background identifiers.

## Accessibility and inclusion

- Write in plain language and expand specialist acronyms.
- Add meaningful alt text, captions, and transcripts.
- Do not communicate essential information by colour alone.
- Use sufficient contrast and readable type.
- Caption video and describe important visuals.
- Represent women, disabled people, elders, children, converts, and diverse communities with dignity rather than tokenism.

## Assets and quotations

Follow [the repository asset policy](../docs/ASSET_POLICY.md). Record source, creator, rights, attribution, modification, and allowed use. Short quotations still require attribution and must respect source limits.

## Channel review record

For substantial content, record:

- title and version;
- intended audience and channel;
- product behavior verified on;
- sources and observation dates;
- asset rights status;
- privacy review;
- cultural or religious-content review;
- professional review where needed;
- approver role and publication date;
- correction contact and revision history.

## Corrections

Correct material promptly and transparently. Preserve a short note when a correction changes a factual, safety, legal, religious, or product-capability claim. Do not silently rewrite consequential errors.
