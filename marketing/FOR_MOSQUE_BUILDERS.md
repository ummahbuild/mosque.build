# For communities building a mosque

## Building a mosque should not be a mystery

A mosque may begin with a simple and sincere intention. Bringing it into lasting service requires many connected decisions: community need, governance, site, qibla, access, design, engineering, approvals, cost, funding, sourcing, construction, opening, and care.

mosque.build gives your committee a place to organize that journey. It helps you prepare better questions and preserve decisions while keeping qualified professionals and authorities responsible for regulated conclusions.

## Begin before buying land

The earliest work is not choosing a dome or commissioning a rendering. It is understanding the institution your community needs.

Record:

- daily, Friday, Ramadan, Eid, and funeral prayer needs;
- women’s, family, youth, elder, and disabled worshippers’ journeys;
- learning, welfare, counselling, administration, and community programmes;
- expected growth and realistic phasing;
- governance and decision responsibilities;
- operating income, staffing, cleaning, utilities, maintenance, and reserves;
- the evidence and professional help needed next.

This brief becomes a test for every later site and design option.

## See the full journey

### 1. Define the need

Agree what the mosque must enable, who it serves, who decides, and what success will look like after opening.

### 2. Understand the place

Compare land or an existing building. Investigate ownership, planning context, access, utilities, soil, drainage, flood and heat risk, neighbours, transport, emergency access, and room for responsible growth.

### 3. Shape the mosque

Coordinate qibla and prayer rows with entrances, columns, accessible positions, wudu, learning, family needs, acoustics, daylight, ventilation, services, landscape, cost, and maintenance.

### 4. Prepare approvals

Identify likely requirements from current authority sources. Assign owners, documents, questions, submission dates, responses, and inspections. A checklist is not an approval; verify the exact site and scope locally.

### 5. Build the financial plan

Connect the community brief to quantities, sourced rates, exclusions, contingency, cash flow, fundraising restrictions, opening costs, operations, and reserves. Do not treat a planning estimate as a quotation.

### 6. Source responsibly

Write clear scopes, compare equivalent offers, inspect samples, verify technical evidence, disclose conflicts, and calculate full delivered and installed cost—not only a headline price.

### 7. Coordinate construction

Keep questions, product approvals, changes, inspections, evidence, payments, progress, commissioning, training, warranties, and handover connected to the decisions that created them.

### 8. Open and care

Opening day begins the building’s operating life. Prepare asset records, maintenance, work orders, emergency plans, staff training, feedback, reserves, and post-occupancy learning.

## What your first session can achieve

In mosque.build, a committee can create a private project brief, identify major gaps, explore relevant planning areas, and leave with a clearer next action. The current public workspace saves drafts in the active browser unless the user exports them.

## Questions to bring to the first committee meeting

1. Which community needs are evidenced, and which are assumptions?
2. Who can recommend, review, decide, and communicate each major choice?
3. What information would make a site commitment unsafe or premature?
4. How will women, children, elders, and disabled worshippers experience the whole journey?
5. What will the mosque cost to operate and maintain after opening?
6. Which decisions require an architect, engineer, planner, authority, legal adviser, quantity surveyor, contractor, or religious reviewer?
7. How will the next committee understand why today’s decisions were made?
