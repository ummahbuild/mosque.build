## Core message

**Building a mosque should not be a mystery.**

Most Muslim communities know how to fundraise.

The harder problem is everything around the fundraiser:

**land → community need → architecture → qibla → parking → engineering → permits → cost → fundraising → sourcing → contractors → construction → inspections → handover → maintenance → waqf**

The article introduces mosque.build as an operating system for bringing mosques to life—not an AI toy that generates pretty renderings.

# LinkedIn — founder post

Muslims are very good at responding to one sentence:

**“We need to build a mosque.”**

Money gets raised. Land gets donated. A rendering appears.

Then the difficult part begins.

Who verified the land can legally support a mosque?

What will Jumu’ah parking actually look like?

Where do women enter?

Can a wheelchair user independently reach prayer and wudu?

How much does the building really cost after MEP, fire, site work, permits, contingency, furniture, AV, commissioning and opening?

Which materials should be sourced locally?

Who owns the next permit response?

Which contractor quote excluded something important?

What happens to every warranty and maintenance manual after the mosque opens?

And how does the masjid pay its operating costs for the next 30 years?

I wrote a long piece laying out the thesis behind **mosque.build**:

> Designing, permitting, funding, building, opening, operating and preserving a mosque should be understandable and accessible to every Muslim community.

Not DIY architecture.

Not AI pretending to be an engineer.

A guided operating system that helps communities understand the process, coordinate professionals, compare decisions, see costs, navigate permits, source products, manage construction, preserve transparency and retain institutional memory.

The spiritual motivation is ancient.

The Prophet ﷺ said that whoever builds a mosque for Allah is promised a house in Paradise.

The coordination layer can be new.

The bigger idea:

**Every mosque should be locally rooted, professionally delivered, transparently stewarded, sustainably operated and globally assisted.**

And every mosque should make the next one easier.

I go into the full practical workflow—from community need and land due diligence through qibla-aware design, parking, permits, local sourcing, construction, commissioning, maintenance and waqf—in the new article.

**Who is building a mosque right now? I’d especially like feedback from mosque board members, architects, contractors, engineers, planners, imams and donors.**

# 12–15 minute founder video script

## Hook

Muslims are incredibly good at responding to one sentence:

**“We need to build a mosque.”**

Someone donates land.

A fundraiser starts.

An architect makes a rendering.

And then suddenly a volunteer mosque committee is managing one of the most complicated projects it has ever touched.

Zoning.

Parking.

Civil engineering.

Fire code.

Wudu drainage.

Accessibility.

Structural design.

Contractor bids.

Change orders.

Donor restrictions.

Construction schedules.

And nobody has one system showing the whole thing.

That is the problem I think **mosque.build** should solve.

## Part 1 — The mosque is an institution

The first mistake is thinking the mosque is primarily a building.

The building exists so Muslims can worship Allah and build community.

So before somebody asks what the dome should look like, ask:

How many people pray here?

How many youth?

How many children need Islamic education?

What do women need?

What does a disabled worshipper experience from parking all the way to wudu and salah?

Do we need counseling?

Janazah?

Food distribution?

A library?

A community kitchen?

A waqf asset?

That is the brief.

## Part 2 — Islamic operating principles

The Qur’an talks about maintaining and cultivating the mosques of Allah.

It talks about amānah.

It praises shūrā.

And it warns us not to waste.

Those principles have very practical implications for construction.

A donor’s money is an amānah.

A design decision should involve shūrā.

A completely unnecessary imported finish should at least be challenged against the responsibility not to waste resources.

And the mosque needs to be maintained long after the opening ceremony.

## Part 3 — Land before architecture

Before a rendering, the app should ask:

Where is the land?

Can a mosque legally operate there?

Does changing a warehouse into an assembly space trigger new requirements?

What does parking look like on Friday?

Can emergency vehicles reach the site?

Are utilities available?

Is drainage going to cost a fortune?

Can the mosque expand?

That can save months—or years—of pain.

## Part 4 — Design

Now the fun part.

Scan the site.

Draw or import the building.

Apply qibla.

Generate prayer rows.

Show capacity.

Show doors and columns that interrupt rows.

Design women’s space.

Design accessible wudu.

Add classrooms.

Youth.

Community.

Parking.

Gardens.

Then switch architectural language.

Swahili coastal.

Ottoman.

Mamluk.

Maghrebi.

Contemporary.

But with provenance—not one fake “Islamic style” filter.

## Part 5 — Cost follows design

Every change should update cost.

Change carpet?

Cost changes.

Change chandelier?

Structural review and lead time may change.

Change surface parking to a garage?

Land saved, cost and schedule change.

Add a planted façade?

Irrigation, structure and maintenance change.

The key product interaction is Quick Replace.

Compare a product against alternatives and immediately see the impact.

## Part 6 — Permits

Then select the jurisdiction.

The app should show the likely approval path.

Not pretend it has approved your mosque.

Show the authority.

Show the source.

Show what needs verification.

Track every submission and every comment.

If the fire department wants a revision, that becomes a task tied to the drawing.

## Part 7 — Capital

Then fundraising should follow the building.

Instead of a giant abstract target:

Sponsor the wudu stations.

Sponsor a classroom.

Fund geotechnical testing.

Fund the roof.

Fund the accessible entrance.

Fund solar.

Fund the maintenance reserve.

Contribute to the waqf.

Now donors can understand what the capital is doing.

## Part 8 — Construction

Then the model becomes the construction record.

RFIs.

Submittals.

Deliveries.

Inspections.

Photos.

Progress.

Change orders.

Payments.

No more losing the institutional history because one project manager leaves.

## Part 9 — Opening and operations

Finally, commission the building.

Run Jumu’ah mode.

Run wudu peak.

Run the audio.

Test the accessible route.

Collect the warranties.

Create the asset register.

Then opening day becomes the beginning of operations—not the end of the project.

## Closing

The Prophet ﷺ promised an extraordinary reward for building a mosque for Allah.

Technology does not make that promise bigger.

But technology can allow more Muslims to participate in the work with competence, transparency and amānah.

And maybe the most important thing:

Every mosque we finish should leave lessons that make the next one easier.

That is what I want **mosque.build** to become.

# Repost / quote-post variants

### Architect
This is where mosque.build gets interesting to me: the goal is not to eliminate professional architecture. It is to give communities better briefs, clearer decisions and better coordination so architects can do better work.

### Engineer
The right role for AI in mosque construction is not “approve the structure.” It is surfacing assumptions, missing inputs, conflicts and professional-review gates.

### Mosque board member
A volunteer mosque committee should not need to become a construction-management company overnight.

That is the coordination problem mosque.build is trying to solve.

### Donor
Transparency should mean more than publishing “we raised $2M.”

Show what was designed, approved, purchased, installed, inspected and still needs funding.

### Contractor
Standardized mosque RFQs could save everybody time: clearer scope, fewer assumptions, normalized bid comparisons, tracked exclusions and cleaner submittals.

### Community
The mosque should be designed by the community it will actually serve—not only the people sitting in the first construction meeting.
