# Islamic architecture library expansion

Status: `source_backed` for historical context; `concept` or `specified` for contemporary application studies.

This increment broadens the library beyond its initial Persian, Mamluk, Ottoman and Sudano-Sahelian coverage. It adds:

- Anatolian timber hypostyle architecture, sourced to UNESCO;
- a western Islamic hypostyle study grounded in UNESCO's Córdoba record;
- an Indo-Islamic courtyard and screen study grounded in UNESCO's Qutb complex record;
- timber-bay, arcade-rhythm and zellij module studies; and
- timber structure, repairable mosaic tile, carved mineral finish and stone lattice assembly studies.

Each historical record identifies spatial principles, climate and material questions, design moves, false shortcuts, compatible studies, provenance, observation date, rights and confidence. Each material assembly names evidence and maintenance obligations rather than asserting suitability, availability or performance.

The continued source expansion adds Kairouan, Lamu, the Maldives coral-stone tentative-list record, Bagerhat, Djenné, the documented Teluk Memali timber-mosque relocation and the Tabačica Mosque restoration in Mostar. These records broaden the product from form comparison into stewardship: recurring earthen maintenance, flood and salt exposure, lawful material sourcing, component-level reuse, compact adaptive space and post-conflict reconstruction ethics are now explicit design questions.

The comparison workspace is now executable rather than read-only. A project team can search spatial and environmental principles, compare up to three records, document why they may be relevant, record unresolved local questions, assign an accountable review role, save the brief on-device and export it with the complete cited records and limitations. The versioned precedent brief is included in whole-project package backup and restore.

The studies are not style templates, reconstructions, sacred-art guidance or construction specifications. Cultural authorship, local craft, religious setting, accessibility, fire, structure, climate and maintenance remain review responsibilities.
